## 1. Item (iii) — temporal/dynamical lag: directly, explicitly addressed — `[VERIFIED-PDF p.18]`

`FINDING_P160` §2 point 3 named this as "a third, genuinely distinct and
untested case... whether a *single, fixed* pair's own induced-dipole
axis tracks the separation direction *without lag* as the pair's
relative configuration evolves over time." v82's own Sec. IV.H, "Formal
boundary at high relative velocity" (p.18), reads in full:

> "This framework is explicitly Newtonian (Sec. IV.O). Realistic node
> velocities are far below c, so we do not regard this — or the notion
> of retarded times — as practically important at present, but note it
> for completeness as a formal limit of the approach."

This is a direct hit: TJB himself names "the notion of retarded times"
— precisely the mechanism a temporal lag would require — and states it
is not practically important given realistic node velocities, framing
it as a formal (not practical) limit tied to the framework's own
explicitly-Newtonian character.

**A secondary cross-check, corrected to its proper, weaker weight
(skeptic-caught gap (e)):** the first draft also argued v82's own
dipole-axis picture (Sec. IV.F, already quoted in `FINDING_P160` §2)
*defines* the axis as "the line connecting the two nodes" — a direct,
instantaneous geometric identification with no relaxation time to lag.
Re-read directly, that passage is explicitly hedged: "one might consider
that... the line connecting the two nodes... supplies the direction" —
a tentative aside offered to resolve a different tension (an isotropic
quantity contributing to a dipole-like force), not a firm definitional
commitment TJB stands behind unconditionally. This secondary argument is
therefore weaker than first presented and is recorded here only as a
suggestive, not load-bearing, cross-check. v82's own force-law equations
(Eqs. 1-4, `FINDING_P159` §2) are, independently and more solidly,
algebraic functions of the *current* separation `s`, `r_A`, `r_P` — no
retarded-time argument appears anywhere in their written form — which is
consistent with, though does not by itself prove, TJB's own direct
statement.

**Item (iii) is closed on the strength of v82's own direct, unhedged
"retarded times... formal limit" quote alone** — the load-bearing
citation — not by new derivation and not by the weaker geometric
cross-check above, which is included only as a consistent, non-decisive
observation. A separate possible "lag" mechanism this file does not
consider or rule out — a finite intracluster-medium sound-crossing or
pressure-response time, distinct from both gravitational retardation and
an EM-style polarization relaxation time — remains genuinely
unexamined, though it is a narrower point than what `P160` originally
asked about.

## 2. Item (ii) — real-node anisotropy: elevated to a real, TJB-acknowledged, still-unquantified gap (corrected, not "closed") — `[VERIFIED-PDF p.17]`

`FINDING_P160` §2 point 2 left open "whether a real, physical node
(finite thermal motion, not a literal ideal point-dipole) has *zero
variance* around that nominal axis." v82's own Sec. IV.H, two adjacent
passages (p.17), address this directly from two angles:

> "**Non-monopole internal mass distribution.** Each node's own mass is
> treated as if concentrated at a point for the purpose of the force
> law, even though clusters are not spherical; typical axis ratios are
> well below unity, and cluster shape and alignment have their own
> established literature. A node with a large internal non-monopole
> moment of its own mass distribution is not equivalent, even
> approximately, to the point-like source this framework assumes."

> "**Non-monopole bulk kinematics.** The dipole term rests on thermal,
> not bulk, kinetic energy dominating a node's ICM (Sec. IV.F). This is
> not a static assumption: non-thermal pressure support is measured to
> roughly double, from about 10 percent to about 45 percent of total
> pressure at `r_200c`, between `z=0` and `z=1.5`. This is real,
> quantified evolution across almost the whole redshift range this paper
> uses, not a concern confined to its high-z end."

These directly confirm `P160`'s own worry is real: TJB's own text states
plainly that real clusters are non-spherical (axis ratios well below
unity) and that the thermal-energy-dominance assumption underlying the
dipole mechanism's own physical picture is measurably z-dependent, not a
fixed property of real nodes. **This does not resolve the question — it
remains real, open, and unquantified.** What this file adds is narrower:
this project does not need to independently establish that the
idealization is imperfect for real nodes, because TJB has already stated
so himself, explicitly, twice, in his own limitations catalogue.
**Item (ii) is NOT closed** — it is correctly re-described as a directly
TJB-acknowledged limitation rather than an internal, unsourced worry;
quantifying either effect's actual impact on the dipole term would need
real cluster-shape and cluster-pressure-profile data this project does
not have, matching `activeContext.md`'s own prior assessment, and remains
as open after this file as before it.

that introduce the same `κ_A≠κ_P` problem `P160` already proved breaks
MULTING's own single-coefficient dipole form?

**The answer depends on scope, and v82's own text settles which scope
this project has actually used.** v82's own p.6 "identical 'typical
node' case we adopt for computational purposes (`m_A=m_P, r_A=r_P,
k_A=k_P`)..." line — the citation the first draft of this file used —
considered as a candidate citation for a
*general* framework-wide claim, does not carry that weight on its own —
it sits inside Sec. II.E, deriving specifically the non-isotropic
mass-accretion correction, not a general statement about how every node
pair in the framework is modeled. **The actually general, correctly-
scoped evidence is Eq. (10) itself** (Sec. II.D, p.6):

> "For X being either A or P, we model node mass using an empirical
> power law... relative to a low-redshift baseline `m_0`:
> `m_X(z) = m_0·(1+z)^(-1.1)`"

`[VERIFIED-PDF]`: this is a **single** baseline `m_0`, with `X` ranging
over `{A, P}` — no per-node subscript (`m_0,A`, `m_0,P`) anywhere in the
definition — so `m_A(z) = m_P(z)` follows directly from v82's own
general evolution law, for *any* pair, at *any* shared redshift `z`, not
merely as a convenience adopted for one derivation. `r_X(z)` and `k_X(z)`
(Eqs. 11-14) are each derived deterministically from this same `m_X(z)`
(p.6-7), so the same identity `r_A(z)=r_P(z)`, `k_A(z)=k_P(z)` follows
throughout v82's entire evolution-law machinery, framework-wide.

**Confirmed to be the actual scope of v82's own fitted point, not merely
a modeling convenience elsewhere ignored:** Sec. II.G, "Computational
details" (p.11), states the framework's three free parameters
(`β1, β2, H0,anchor`) "are determined by chi-squared minimization of the
integrated `H(z)` curve... against the 31 Cosmic Chronometer data
points, the SH0ES point, and the DESI point (33 total)" — real
*observational* `H(z)` measurements, never against individually-
differentiated real cluster-pair properties. v82's own actual fit
therefore never leaves the identical-typical-node scope either — this is
not a simplification this project's own reconstruction (`P156`-`P163`)
introduced on top of a more general v82 construction; it is the scope
v82's own published fit itself operates in throughout.

`[VERIFIED-logic]`: if `m_A=m_P`, `k_A=k_P`, `r_A=r_P` — i.e. node A and
node P share *identical* values of every property any function `κ(x)`
could possibly depend on, at the same redshift `z` — then for **any**
function `κ`, however node-property-dependent, `κ(x_A)=κ(x_P)` trivially,
since `x_A=x_P` by construction. `FINDING_P160`'s own §1 result (two
different coefficients `2κ_A/c²`, `2κ_P/c²` arise from the dipole tier
unless `κ_A=κ_P`) is therefore automatically satisfied for **any**
`κ(x)` within this scope — not just a constant `κ`. **`FINDING_P160`'s
own "genuinely open" framing for item (i) was correct as stated (the
question is real for the general case), but the specific tension it
worried about — that node-dependent `κ(x)` might break MULTING's dipole
form the way a badly-chosen asymmetric coupling would — does not arise
within the representative/typical-node scope this project's own work has
actually used.** Beyond that scope — real, individually-diverse cluster
pairs, which v82's own Sec. IV.H "Typical objects, not distributions"
caveat (§2 above, already used by `P157`) explicitly flags as
unrepresented and unquantified by v82's own current construction — the
question remains as open as `P160` originally stated, for exactly the
reason v82's own text gives, not a new one this file introduces.
