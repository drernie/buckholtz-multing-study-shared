## Step 8a skeptic — context-blind, claim + data only — verdict: WEAKENED

Five real, substantive problems, all accepted (none dismissed):

1. **MCID was null-preserving by construction.** A conjunctive
   `(≥2× mean) AND (≤1 counter-example)` rule, applied to a small,
   heterogeneous literature sample, fails almost regardless of the true
   effect — any single qualitative "good agreement" report anywhere in
   Group B triggers automatic failure on condition (b), independent of
   its sample size or how directly comparable it is. **Accepted as a
   real design flaw**, not a defensible pre-registration.
2. **Bias and scatter are not commensurable and were mixed as one
   "disagreement magnitude."** Applegate's `4%` and Simet's `25%` are
   systematic mean offsets; Hoekstra's `12%` is a scatter (random
   spread), not a bias; `P197`'s own `2.5×` bundles a mean ratio AND a
   `74.5%` scatter into one headline number. Comparing these on one axis
   is a category error. **Accepted, real, load-bearing.**
3. **Group B was 75% self-citation.** 3 of 4 Group-B entries
   (`P196`-`P198`, `P197`, `P200`) are this project's own prior findings,
   not independent external literature — they share the same mass
   definitions, cluster samples, and methodology choices as each other.
   Calling this "backed by 5 additional independent sources" overstated
   the independence; only Diaferio 2005 is a genuinely external Group-B
   data point. **Accepted.**
4. **Diaferio (N=3, qualitative, 2005) was given equal veto weight
   against P197 (N=123, quantitative, this project's own real HeCS-SZ
   data).** Sample-size-blind counter-example rules have this failure
   mode generally. When the skeptic recomputed the group means *without*
   this asymmetry (i.e., not letting one N=3 anecdote veto a pattern
   visible across `N=123+44+`dozens of clusters), Group B's mean
   disagreement **exceeds** Group A's by roughly `10-15×` — comfortably
   clearing the original `≥2×` threshold. **Accepted as the single most
   important correction** — it reverses which conclusion the same raw
   data actually supports.
5. **Search coverage bias and unaddressed confounds**: SZ appears on
   both sides (Hoekstra's WL-vs-SZ in Group A, `P197`'s caustic-vs-SZ in
   Group B) without being flagged as a possible shared driver; overdensity
   radius (`r_2500` vs `r_200`/`r_500`) was not held constant across
   entries; well-known higher-bias WL-vs-X-ray studies (von der Linden
   et al. 2014, `Weighing the Giants III`; Israel et al. 2014; Sereno &
   Ettori) were not found by the 2 bounded search queries and would have
   widened Group A's own spread. **Accepted as real, unresolved gaps** —
   not corrected here (would require additional bounded search effort
   beyond today's remaining scope).

**No point was dismissed.** Per the Response Matrix, this is a genuine
`WEAKENED` — the underlying observation survives, narrower and
differently framed, not `FALSIFIED` outright.

## Corrected, honest statement (replaces the Group A/B framing)

**Withdrawing** the formal "Group A vs Group B mean, MCID-gated"
comparison — it does not survive scrutiny as constructed (points 1-3
above). **What actually holds, stated narrowly:**

- Published, independent, external WL-vs-X-ray-hydrostatic bias
  measurements cluster in the **`4%-26%`** systematic-offset range
  (Applegate, Simet, Muñoz-Echeverría), with a separately-reported
  **`~12%`** scatter figure (Hoekstra, WL-vs-SZ) that is a different
  statistical quantity and not merged into that range.
- `P197` (this project's own real HeCS-SZ measurement, caustic mass vs
  Planck SZ mass, `N=123`, `[VERIFIED-BASH]`) found a `2.5×` mean ratio
  with `74.5%` scatter — **well outside** the published WL-vs-X-ray bias
  range above, on both the offset and (where comparable) the scatter
  axis.
- The one genuinely external, caustic-specific counter-datapoint found
  (Diaferio et al. 2005, caustic vs WL, `N=3`) reported close agreement
  — but at `N=3`, hand-selected as a caustic-method validation sample,
  it is weak evidence against `P197`'s `N=123`, not a rebuttal.

**Honest conclusion:** `P197`'s own caustic-vs-SZ disagreement sits at
the extreme end of, or beyond, the range spanned by published,
independent, non-caustic cluster-mass-proxy comparisons — consistent
with (not proof of) the `[HYPOTHESIS]` already named in the withdrawn
draft: that caustic/dynamical-estimator comparisons may carry larger
disagreement than lensing-anchored ones, for reasons (sample
heterogeneity, projection/interloper contamination in caustic
identification) not tested here. This is a real, useful sharpening of
`P202`'s own `PART-OF-MACROSYSTEM` verdict — a plausible, testable
`[HYPOTHESIS]`, not the disqualified formal sub-rule.
