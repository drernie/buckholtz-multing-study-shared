## The pre-registered gate **failed** — and the failure was in the gate

Part D demanded `<2 %` change over the final stretch. `G` instead rises
monotonically: at `k=10`, `1.072 → 1.150 → 1.234 → 1.315`, a final change of
**6.55 %**.

**The reason is structural, not numerical.** `G` is growth **accumulated** over a
window `[a₁,a₂]`. *If* the force induces a **persistent rate shift**, an
accumulated ratio keeps rising with the window — it is window-dependent by
construction, and demanding convergence of it was a category error in the gate.

> **Qualified after review.** An earlier draft said "**must**". That is too
> strong: a force that boosts growth *transiently* and then saturates would give
> a **convergent** `G` and an `ε` tending to zero. The categorical phrasing
> dressed a conclusion as a necessity. The honest statement is conditional — and
> the condition is testable, which is what makes `ε` falsifiable.

The window-free quantity is the growth-index shift:

```
ε(k) := d ln G / d ln a          so    G = (a₂/a₁)^ε
```

| k | ε @10⁵ | @10⁶ | @10⁷ | @8×10⁷ | spread |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.1 | 0.002655 | 0.001142 | 0.001191 | 0.001421 | **2.33×** |
| 1.0 | 0.024499 | 0.024649 | 0.024742 | 0.024793 | **1.0120×** |
| 10 | 0.045472 | 0.045596 | 0.045657 | 0.045688 | **1.0047×** |

`ε` is constant to **0.47 %** at `k=10` across four decades in `a`, and **is not**
constant at `k=0.1`. The window dependence of `G` is fully explained by a constant
rate shift wherever a rate exists.
