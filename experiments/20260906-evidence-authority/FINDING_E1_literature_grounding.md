## Result: a mature, decades-deep subfield, with a name

This is not open ground. Three converging research programs, all
`[VERIFIED-arXiv]`, all doing some version of exactly what TJB describes:

1. **"Cosmography" (Weinberg's term, per Visser 2004)** — deliberately
   separates cosmological *kinematics* (what a Taylor expansion of the
   scale factor around "here and now" directly gives) from cosmological
   *dynamics* (the Einstein/Friedmann field equations and whatever
   stress-energy content — dark energy, dark matter — is needed to
   satisfy them). Matt Visser, **"Cosmography: Cosmology without the
   Einstein equations"** (arXiv:gr-qc/0411131, 2004): *"How much of
   modern cosmology is really cosmography? How much of modern cosmology
   is independent of the Einstein equations?... it behoves us to step
   back a little and carefully disentangle cosmological kinematics from
   cosmological dynamics."* This is TJB's own question, in near-identical
   language, 20+ years earlier. Active review: Dunsby & Luongo (2015),
   **"On the theory and applications of modern cosmography"**
   (arXiv:1511.06532) — *"aims to describe the universe without the need
   of postulating a priori any particular cosmological model."*
2. **FLRW/Copernican-principle consistency tests** — Clarkson, Bassett &
   Lu, **"A general test of the Copernican Principle"** (arXiv:0712.3457,
   2007), the seminal paper; Uzan, Clarkson & Ellis, **"Time drift of
   cosmological redshifts as a test of the Copernican principle"**
   (arXiv:0801.0068, 2007); still active, e.g. Euclid Collaboration
   (arXiv:2207.09995, 2022) planning next-generation-survey tests. Most
   directly relevant: **Shafieloo & Clarkson, "Model independent tests
   of the standard cosmological model"** (arXiv:0911.4858, 2009) — uses
   `H(z)` measurements *specifically* (the same observable v82's own
   bridge targets) to test the Copernican principle and homogeneity
   assumption "independent of dark energy or metric based theory of
   gravity."
3. **Cosmic distance-duality relation (CDDR) tests** — a large,
   still-active sub-literature (papers found spanning 2011-2026)
   testing `D_L(z)(1+z)^-2/D_A(z)=1` model-independently, using paired,
   independent distance measures (SNe Ia + galaxy clusters, or SNe Ia +
   radio quasars) precisely so no specific cosmological model needs to
   be assumed to detect a violation.
