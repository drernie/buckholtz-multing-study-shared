# Note for Dr. Buckholtz — beta_d/beta_q Profiling Result (Cosmological Branch)

**Date:** 2026-07-13
**Scope:** independent verification only, not a claim about the theory itself.
**Labels:** NOT_VALIDATION · NOT_REFUTATION · OUR_RECONSTRUCTION · NOT_AUTHOR_ERROR

Dear Dr. Buckholtz,

As with everything I send you, I am not claiming co-authorship or any share of
credit — this is independent verification work, offered for your use however you
see fit.

**What I checked.** In my reconstruction of the cosmological branch (the
dipole/quadrupole modification to gravity tested against real galaxy-cluster and
cosmic-chronometer H(z) data), I ran a full profile over the two free coefficients
(beta_d, beta_q) and compared every configuration against the simplest nested case
— the model with both coefficients set to zero (a plain inverse-square term).

**What I found.** In my implementation, tuning `(beta_d, beta_q)` — at any scale I
tested, and in the limit where the quadrupole term grows without bound — does not
improve the fit to real H(z) data relative to that plain baseline. An earlier
"best fit" I had reported (r≈0.62) turned out to come from a search that never
actually tried the simplest case (both coefficients at zero, r≈0.73) — once I
included it, the simpler case won. I also derived, in closed form, why increasing
the quadrupole coefficient without bound always settles on the same fixed number
(0.623517...): in that limit the coefficients drop out of the prediction entirely,
and it becomes a fit using only the catalog data itself, with no free parameters
left.

**What this does NOT mean.** I am not saying the underlying theory is
mathematically excluded, and I am not saying anything here reflects an error on
your part — this is a property of my own reconstruction of the `F -> H(z)` step
(the mapping from the pairwise force law to an observable expansion rate), which
was always a phenomenological placeholder on my end (`D = D0/(1+z)`), not a
derivation from your action or field equations. What I can say is: in my
reproducible pipeline, no amount of adjusting `(beta_d, beta_q)` recovers or beats
the plain baseline.

**Why I think this is useful to you:**
- It is a precise statement of a limitation in the existing fit, not a vague one —
  I can tell you exactly which range of coefficients I checked and why none of them
  help.
- It is a ready, reproducible negative result — every number below is generated by
  one script, so a reviewer (or you) can re-run it directly.
- It comes with a clear condition for reopening the question: if a physically
  derived `F -> H(z)` mapping becomes available (rather than my placeholder), or an
  independent constraint pins down `(beta_d, beta_q)` from theory, this test should
  be re-run against that — not against more coefficient-fitting in my current
  pipeline.
- It does not claim to rule out MULTING as a theory — only that this specific
  rescue strategy, inside this specific implementation, does not work.
