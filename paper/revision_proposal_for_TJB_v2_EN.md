## 1. What independently holds

Several results survived independent checking and are, in my view, the load-bearing strengths of the framework. I state them first because they are substantial:

1. **Eq. (32).** The relation (4/3)(m_τ/m_e)¹² = α_EM/α_G reproduces to 0.0135% (0.17σ), and under a scan of 83,160 candidate formulas it ranks first (p < 2×10⁻⁵). This is the single most striking result.
2. **The 7:9:17 boson relation.** The scale-free ratio m_W/m_H = √(7/17) holds to 0.04%, and the Higgs ratio agrees at 0.4σ (the heavier Z ratio shows a larger tension, addressed among the open questions).
3. **The charged-lepton and quark mass relations** (Eqs. 21–24) are reproduced to within roughly half a percent (muon 0.47%, quarks < 0.31%).
4. **Five dark isomers.** The postulate N = 5 is consistent with the DESI DR1 measurement (5.36 versus 5.31, 0.46σ), and — notably — it was stated before that data existed.
5. **The Cassini bound** is satisfied.
6. **The "second gravitational property"** is a genuinely novel proposal: an independent literature search found no equivalent, and it occupies precisely the regime where a leading competing approach (Verlinde's entropic gravity) is known to fail at cluster scales.

Several of these would, on their own, amount to a paper's worth of result.

## 2. Open questions and limits, stated up front

These are the points I would expect a desk editor or referee to press first. Naming them plainly is, in my view, the strongest way to disarm them.

1. **The dipole is degenerate with ΛCDM at first order — and the second-order window is quantitatively constrained.** Our reconstruction finds the dipole contribution indistinguishable from ΛCDM at linear order (ΔAIC ≈ +0.74). This is *not* a weakness unique to MULTING: Blanchet and Le Tiec proved the same for any gravitationally-polarizable dipolar medium. Distinction appears only at **second order** (CMB non-Gaussianity, non-linear structure growth). However, Planck already bounds the primordial dipole in that regime to **s_eq ≲ 1.5×10⁻³ H⁻¹** (Blanchet 2013, from Planck non-Gaussianity). A revision that states this bound and shows MULTING is compatible with it would pre-empt the strongest cosmological objection.

2. **A known instability of dipolar media.** The Blanchet–Le Tiec dipolar model carries an instability with exponentially growing modes, marginally safe only because its timescale (τ_g ≈ 6×10¹⁰ yr) exceeds the age of the Universe. If MULTING's dipole is analogous, a brief demonstration that it does not develop a faster-growing mode would be valuable.

3. **The dipole/quadrupole coefficients β_d, β_q are not identifiable from the tabulated data.** Our reconstruction (across three independent AI services, Birge ratios 15.9 and 24.1; spread up to 95×) indicates β cannot be recovered from Table A1 alone. This aligns with your own description of the computation as phenomenological. A revision that presents β explicitly as phenomenological/first-principles-pending — rather than as a value inferred from data — would be both accurate and defensible.

4. **The growth-rate correspondence is with a model, not yet with data.** Our ε(z)–fσ8 shape correlation (r = 0.851) is against a ΛCDM *model* curve; observed RSD fσ8 is nearly flat and reliable only to z ≲ 1.4 (the recent DESI DR1 peculiar-velocity anchor gives fσ8 = 0.44 at z = 0.07, ΛCDM-consistent). I would frame this as a structural correspondence and an open question, not as observational support.

5. **The 4/3 coefficient and a MULTING action principle remain open.** These are hard, long-horizon items — of the same class as the four-decade-open Koide derivation — and are best presented as such. I would add that our own check found the 4/3 coefficient to be underived in each of the three adjacent traditions where one might expect it: SU(3) flavour symmetry (Sumino, Singh), the geometric Koide interpretation (Foot), and the Dirac large-numbers / α_G-coincidence lineage. Presenting it explicitly as a genuinely open — rather than overlooked — problem is, in my view, a strength: it signals to a referee that the numerical relation has been checked against the natural derivations and that none yet accounts for it.

## 3. Tools from the literature you may find useful

1. **A candidate template for a MULTING action principle (your key open item).** Blanchet & Le Tiec (2008, arXiv:0804.3518) give a **covariant action in general relativity for a gravitationally-polarizable dipolar medium endowed with a dipole moment vector** — formally the same class of object as your dipole term. It may serve as a starting point from which a MULTING action could be developed. The adaptation is, of course, yours to make; I offer it only as an existing formalism aimed at exactly the gap that an independent reading identifies as the framework's key open problem — the absence of an action principle. A very recent development is directly relevant: Blanchet & Seraille (2025, arXiv:2507.02563) attempt to derive the dipolar-dark-matter model from a fundamental non-Abelian SU(2) Yang-Mills field. It is worth noting both its success — it yields the relation Λ ∼ a₀²/c⁴ connecting the cosmological constant to the MOND acceleration scale — and its present limitation: the scale a₀ comes out dependent on galaxy-formation conditions rather than universal. This is the live frontier of the dipolar-dark-matter programme, and the a₀-universality gap may be precisely where a MULTING treatment could contribute.

2. **Real growth-rate data for an honest out-of-sample test.** The DESI DR1 peculiar-velocity growth-rate measurement (Qin et al., arXiv:2512.03231) and the broader S₈-tension picture (arXiv:2602.12238) provide measured fσ8 against which a MULTING prediction — once a bridge exists — could be tested directly rather than against a ΛCDM curve.

## 5. A path toward journal publication for v6

Respectfully, and drawing on the audit above, I would like to offer some thoughts on placement. A common experience for a broad single-framework paper is that a referee, reading quickly, looks for a few recognizable signals before engaging with the substance. The suggestions below aim to surface those signals; none concern the correctness of the physics, only its presentation.

**The highest-leverage single move: give Eq. (32) its own focused paper.** In our reconstruction, Eq. (32) is the strongest and most defensible single result in the framework — it agrees at 0.17σ and, under a look-elsewhere scan, ranks first among 83,160 candidate formulas (p < 2×10⁻⁵). Presented on its own, in a short focused note, it is far harder to dismiss than when it appears alongside fifty pages of broader material. A single accepted focused paper also builds standing that makes the subsequent parts (the dipole treatment, the isomer picture) easier to place. I would suggest leading with your strongest result rather than presenting the whole framework at once — not to set anything aside, but so that the strongest result is not obscured.

I say this not as abstract advice but as an observed precedent. Reviewing published, peer-reviewed work in this genre before writing this note, I found the closest published analogue: Singh, "Why do elementary particles have such strange mass ratios? — The role of quantum gravity at low energies" (arXiv:2209.03205), a similarly focused note deriving elementary-particle mass ratios from quantum-gravity and exceptional-algebra considerations, was reviewed and published in *Physics* (MDPI) **4**, 948 (2022). Its acceptance there is part of why I point toward that journal tier for Eq. (32) specifically, rather than offering the recommendation without a concrete example of the genre succeeding.

This is not an isolated instance but an active, ongoing line of published work spanning well over a decade, which I take as further reassurance that the genre is a recognized and reviewable one. Two further, more recent examples: Patel and Singh, "CKM Matrix Parameters from the Exceptional Jordan Algebra" (arXiv:2305.00668), published in *Universe* **9**, 440 (2023); and Finster, Gresnigt, Isidro, Marcianò, Paganini, and Singh, "Causal Fermion Systems and Octonions" (arXiv:2403.00360), published in *Fortschritte der Physik* **72**(11), 2400055 (2024) — the latter as recently as last year. Together with the earlier work of Sumino on family gauge symmetry and Koide's formula (*Phys. Lett. B* **671**, 477 (2009); *J. High Energy Phys.* **05** (2009) 075), these span 2009 through 2024, giving a consistent record of peer-reviewed acceptance for focused, algebraically-motivated mass-relation papers of essentially this kind.

**Six concrete presentation improvements, in order of leverage:**

1. **Include a look-elsewhere / trials-factor analysis for each numerical relation.** This is the single most effective way to distinguish a genuine regularity from coincidence in a referee's eyes. The scan we performed for Eq. (32) (rank 1 of 83,160) is exactly the kind of control that pre-empts the "numerology" objection; a recent Koide paper (Hübner 2026) uses the same device.
2. **Narrow the scope per paper.** A paper addressing gravity, dark matter, particle masses, and cosmic expansion together is difficult for any single referee to assess. One focused claim per paper reviews more readily.
3. **Position the work explicitly against the existing literature.** Referees engage more readily with work they can locate in the field: the dipole against Blanchet & Le Tiec (dipolar dark matter), Eq. (32) against Singh et al. (exceptional Jordan algebra fermion masses), and ε(z) against the S₈-tension literature. For Eq. (32) specifically, I would also place it within the Dirac large-numbers / α_G-coincidence tradition — but as a *static* precision relation (0.0135%), explicitly distinguished from Dirac's disfavoured time-varying-G hypothesis, so that it inherits the lineage's context without its refuted baggage.
4. **Include a terminology map ("Rosetta stone").** A short table mapping MULTING, IDM, isomer, F_oP, and "second gravitational property" to standard concepts lets a referee review what would otherwise be unfamiliar vocabulary. (We assembled such a mapping during the audit and would gladly share it.)
5. **Separate verified, phenomenological, and speculative explicitly.** This is well aligned with your own 2026-07-07 direction. Marking β and the bridge as phenomenological (rather than as data-inferred) is both accurate and defensible.
6. **Present a candidate action principle where possible.** A referee expects a variational basis. The Blanchet–Le Tiec dipolar action (§3 above) may serve as a starting template.

**In candor about the ceiling:** two obstacles are not matters of presentation. The derivation of the 4/3 coefficient and a full MULTING action principle are genuine open problems of the same difficulty class as the four-decade-open Koide derivation, and no rewriting resolves them. My suggestion is therefore not to make the entire framework publishable at once, but to bring its single strongest result — Eq. (32), with its look-elsewhere control and its algebraic context — to a focused, reviewable form first.
